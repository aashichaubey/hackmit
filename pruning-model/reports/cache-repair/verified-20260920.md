# Cache repair: verified implementation and fresh benchmark

**Result: about 90% of compressed-history states reused, with median transition
time reduced from 113 ms to 65 ms. This remains approximate, and compression
quality is a blocker: every compressed method answered 4/12 factual questions
correctly, versus 8/12 using the original history.**

This is a fresh local run following review of the existing uncommitted prototype
and its [earlier diagnostic](README.md). Historical results were not pooled with
this run. It is not a benchmark of The Token Company's product.

## Implementation

The [repair module](../../src/pruning_model/cache_repair.py) now exposes
`continue_after_edit(model, original, old_ids, new_ids, query_ids, ...)`:

- By default, copy the identical prefix's KV and recompute the remaining edited
  history together with the incoming query using Qwen's ordinary forward pass.
- Selective repair requires explicit `fraction` and source-token `mapping`
  arguments. The prototype aligns surviving tokens, rotates their cached keys
  into their new positions, and recomputes selected states at every layer.
- New tokens and retokenized joins are always recomputed. The fixed policy
  prioritizes positions close to edits, without seeing the final query or answer.
- The original cache is unchanged. Noncontiguous/evicted source caches and invalid
  mappings/budgets are rejected. Exact inference requires that the source cache
  itself was computed exactly from the supplied original token sequence.

The selective policy is a heuristic, not a trained cache repair model or a
reproduction of CacheBlend. Reused deeper states still reflect the old preceding
text. Rephasing positions alone does not remove that influence.

The [benchmark](../../src/pruning_model/cache_repair_benchmark.py) now exercises
the reusable API, records original-history answers separately, records decode
time and cache tensor storage, and distinguishes reference agreement from
answer correctness.

## Results

Qwen/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, pinned revision
`989aa7980e4cf806f80c7fef2b1adb7bc71aa306`, FP16, Mac MPS, PyTorch 2.14.0,
Transformers 4.57.6. Fourteen public LongMemEval oracle-history examples, seven
strategies, three timed repetitions after a shape-specific warmup per case/arm.
Arm order is randomized with seed 17. All 98 strategy outputs completed; 14
original-history controls and 14 independent reference answers were also generated.
No hosted inference or judge calls were made.

| Strategy | History states reused | Median transition | Answers identical to fresh rebuild | Factual correctness |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Fresh compressed rebuild | 0% | 112.6 ms | 14/14 | 4/12 |
| Exact common-prefix reuse | 13.72% | 102.0 ms | 14/14 | 4/12 |
| Repair mandatory new/boundary tokens | 93.53% | 65.7 ms | 9/14 | 4/12 |
| Repair approximately 10% | 89.95% | 65.0 ms | 10/14 | 4/12 |
| Repair approximately 25% | 74.96% | 74.3 ms | 10/14 | 4/12 |
| Repair approximately 50% | 49.97% | 92.3 ms | 9/14 | 4/12 |
| Full repair, correctness control | 0% | 138.6 ms | 14/14 | 4/12 |

The 10% method reduced the displayed median transition time by **42.3%** versus
a fresh rebuild. The ratio of summed case times was **1.895x**, equivalent to
47.2% less aggregate transition time. Those statistics are different and should
not be interchanged. It was faster than both fresh rebuild and exact prefix
reuse in 11/14 cases; the three shortest histories favored ordinary rebuilding.

Reuse is weighted by tokens in the **compressed history**, not original prompt
length or total model FLOPs. Surviving shifted keys still need rotation/copy work.
All methods ultimately retain the same shortened cache length; this experiment
measures the computation needed to obtain that cache. Original and replacement
caches coexist during repair, so peak memory can rise during transition.

Across cases, histories contained 53,671 original tokens and 10,374 compressed
tokens, with another 705 query tokens. This aggressive compression is identical
across all seven strategy arms. The original-quality control uses the uncompressed
history and is reported separately.

## Correctness versus fidelity

Exact prefix reuse and full repair matched fresh-rebuild first-token logits and
generated token sequences on all 14 cases. Unit tests also compare cache tensors
and continuation logits on a small Qwen model.

The 10% repair method matched the first generated token on 12/14 cases and the
complete generated sequence on 10/14. Its median first-token KL divergence from
fresh rebuilding was 0.0947. At 25% and 50%, this fell to 0.0462 and 0.0152.
These are first-token diagnostics, not a bound on whole-answer error. More repair
did not monotonically increase verbatim answer agreement.

Manual review against the public references found the same four correct factual
answers in every compressed arm: the two abstention questions, number of meetings
with Alex, and Arati Prabhakar. Original history scored eight correct. Five
originally correct answers became incorrect after compression: the mother's
grocery method, Manolo Garcia, daily guitar practice, moving duration, and device
purchase order. Compression also corrected the original model's mistaken
father-gift answer, producing a net loss of four correct answers.

One changed factual response illustrates why reference agreement and correctness
must be separate: partial repair answered three days for Japan plus Chicago,
while fresh rebuilding answered two. Both were wrong; the reference was eleven
or twelve days.

The two preference questions receive qualitative review only. Responses omitted
the requested personal memories and the before-9:30 evening preference. The
evening response hit the 128-token cap for original history, fresh rebuilding,
exact prefix reuse, 25%, 50%, and full repair. Shorter answers in other arms are
not automatically evidence of better performance.

Review was performed by the Codex agent against public references and previously
inspected source evidence. It is not independent human evaluation or the official
LongMemEval judge. The twelve factual items have a low compressed baseline; equal
scores are weak evidence of quality preservation and do not establish equivalence.

## Timing scope and limits

Transition timing includes cache construction/repair plus processing the final
query, and includes source-alignment preparation for selective repair. It excludes
the already-paid original prefill, loading, compression itself, and generation.
Compression outputs were reused from the unchanged 149M pruner's saved run;
the compressor was not rerun or timed here.

The recorded median transition-plus-decode times were 223.3 ms for fresh rebuild,
215.7 ms for exact prefix reuse, and 152.8 ms for 10% repair. These combine a
median transition measurement with a single decode and involve different outputs
(248 generated tokens summed across fresh-rebuild cases versus 122 at 10%). They
are not a controlled equal-output latency comparison or a whole-pipeline saving.

This remains a development diagnostic on fourteen previously inspected oracle
histories, not a fresh holdout or the standard full-history LongMemEval benchmark.
It covers one compaction per case, batch-one full-attention Qwen2 with default
RoPE, and short generations. It does not establish production throughput, peak
system memory savings, multi-compaction stability, other-model behavior, or
hosted-token billing savings.

## Validation and artifacts

All **55 pruning-project tests pass**. New coverage includes exact continuation
after early edits, unchanged history, truncation and append-only changes; source
immutability; suffix-wide repair equivalence; invalid budgets; and rejection of
evicted source caches or missing provenance. Existing tests cover source alignment,
mandatory new-token repair, and no future-question leakage into repair selection.

Artifact verification confirmed 98 unique case/strategy rows, three timing samples
per row, 14 original controls, exact source-snapshot hashes, and unchanged prior
pruning artifacts. Saved data is in
`pruning-model/runs/cache-repair-verified-20260920/` (Git-ignored):

- `manifest.json` and `executed_source/`: pinned runtime, model and executed code.
- `edits.jsonl`: original/compressed text, token IDs and provenance mappings.
- `results.jsonl`, `original_answers.jsonl`, `summary.json`: raw outputs and timings.
- `quality_review.json`: case-by-case review bound to source/result hashes.
- `verified_report.json`: independently checked counts and reviewed metrics.

Reproduce from `pruning-model/`, using a new output directory:

```sh
.venv/bin/python -m pruning_model.cache_repair_benchmark \
  --prior-run runs/kv-longmemeval-20260920 --checkpoint runs/model \
  --output runs/cache-repair-reproduction --limit 14 \
  --repetitions 3 --max-new-tokens 128 --device mps
.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q
```

The result supports continued work on selective repair, but not a production
claim that compression preserves accuracy. The next evaluation should use
better-retained evidence, new conversations, and repeated compaction, while
keeping exact suffix recomputation as the default and correctness reference.
