# What the development loop learned

These observations come from this implementation and its saved development traces. They are not learned model weights, independent review, or a promise of improving on every run.

1. **A source-quote match is necessary but not sufficient.** Early corpus drafts paraphrased quotes. Exact checks rejected them. Later annotation selects numbered source spans, so references remain literal. Semantic annotations are still provisional: a meeting date can be misclassified as a deadline, and a discussion can be mislabeled as a commitment.
2. **An eval must name the task it measures.** Initial public-source questions included background, attendance, statuses, and mismatched yes/no labels. They were retained as a broader, provisional source-question track. A separate deterministic field-preservation track isolates grammar comprehension. Neither replaces independent annotation review.
3. **Aggressive ASCII normalization destroys meaningful differences.** `C++`, `C#`, signed numbers and non-Latin names could collapse under the V1 grader. Evolution uses Unicode normalization, case folding, whitespace folding and terminal-period tolerance while preserving internal symbols. Regression tests cover these collisions.
4. **The shortest local candidates often waste model probes.** Pure token ranking over-selected punctuation grammars. The search now preserves an English anchor, structural diversity, and a previously unseen single-edit neighbor of an accurate parent. Multi-kind crossover is not mislabeled as a single-edit repair.
5. **The stressors have to enter the pilot.** Putting contrasts after all public questions kept them out of early probes. Eight public anchors and eight paired contrasts are now present from the first round. Later rotations can prioritize the development failure archive.
6. **Repairs can help locally and still be poor languages.** A controlled decision-marker edit fixed real model failures and the corresponding synthetic contrasts across multiple source IDs. Other edits introduced regressions and were rejected. Full-development checks remain mandatory; repaired candidates are not automatically deployable.
7. **Unknown is not the same as no.** The reference answerer sometimes interprets a mere proposal as an explicit negative decision. Such responses continue to fail the expected-found check; the grader was not loosened to accept them.
8. **Extraction failure must never expose gold facts.** A regression test ensures missing parsed facts use raw notes rather than an accidental fallback to benchmark gold. This is particularly important when a dictionary lookup returns `None`.
9. **Cached evaluations and paid requests are different accounting units.** A replay preserves historical provider usage for comparison, but never increments actual spending or pretends to be a provider cache hit. Missing usage remains an outstanding conservative reservation. Concurrent commands share transactional limits.
10. **General-purpose behavior requires honest fallback.** Five fact kinds cannot represent every future question about a transcript. The classifier flags unsupported or ambiguous content. The product preserves original notes, uses one selected route, and defaults to raw when no grammar qualifies. Paying for extraction before falling back can be more expensive; reports show that loss.
11. **Dollar budgets do not prevent rate-limit failures.** Validation's larger prompts hit the provider's token-per-minute ceiling despite staying far below $10. Thirty rejected requests remain failed rows with unknown usage. A persisted leaky-bucket schedule now paces research dispatch below the observed rate limit. This infrastructure change was made before test; it changes timing only, with the selected grammar, labels, prompts and fallback policy frozen.
12. **Grounded evidence does not guarantee grounded fields.** A separate development-only successor selected exact source IDs but still paraphrased fields. All four extractions failed validation. Copying complete sentences locally fixed three cases, but broad QA fell to 22/40 versus raw's 28/40. That candidate was rejected despite lower cost; original frozen artifacts were unchanged.
13. **Fallback can masquerade as quality improvement.** The first successor's 28/40 came entirely from raw notes. Reports now separate encoded and fallback scores and count paired regressions. A cheaper extractor and an aggregate score alone cannot demonstrate useful compression.
14. **Small reversible edits can still perturb answers.** A full-source grammar saved 60/118,463 development answer-input tokens without deleting source information, but introduced two exact-score failures. One revealed a missing valid name variant; the other confused discussion with decision. Structural reversibility is not a comprehension guarantee.
15. **Temperature zero is not a stability guarantee.** Five fresh, budgeted repeats per representation on those two failures showed varying raw answers as well. Repeated-call diagnostics preserve every answer and the original pilot; they never pick a lucky result to qualify a candidate. Their narrow, selected sample does not establish population accuracy.

Development artifacts before the suite/probe audits remain in `reports/evolution/search-before-*.json`. Validation and test are protected by one-way locks and frozen hashes. No grammar, extraction rule, or prompt is tuned from their results.

## Published QA successor: observed corrections

* Human references resolve the old review dependency but need native adapters. Multiple spans in one annotation are jointly required; separate annotators are alternatives. Overlapping source intervals must not count the same evidence twice.
* Exact reconstruction and lower tokenizer counts do not imply equal model comprehension. All three 32-question speaker revisions failed quality gates; a 7.26% local inheritance saving also lost an answer in a four-question model diagnostic. Keep failed results, not just the cheapest candidate.
* Report abstention explicitly. Both raw and minimally encoded inputs answered only 2/16 unanswerable cases correctly; an aggregate content-overlap metric alone hid this major task failure.
* The answer-length cap is an experimental variable. Raising it after truncation requires matched raw/candidate conditions and a clearly separate run. Parsing exceptions can leave provider usage unknown; preserve that uncertainty and reservation.
* Finish raw baselines before equivalent fallback candidate jobs. Otherwise concurrent identical failed requests can dispatch twice rather than replay.
* Inherited-speaker quotation decoding needs the complete source context. A standalone `↳` span cannot identify its speaker. Resolve all exact occurrences and reject ambiguous reconstructions without consulting gold labels.
* Strict citation types and range validity matter: Python booleans compare equal to integers. Invalid citation sets must not earn positive reference overlap.
* Published split names do not prevent shared AMI/ICSI meeting families crossing datasets. Track canonical families before any fresh test is opened.
* MISeD lexical response overlap and citation matching measure different things; neither alone proves faithfulness. Reference-history conditioning is not end-to-end dialogue performance.

## User-directed quality tradeoffs and fresh test

The user explicitly allowed modest quality degradation for exploration. We therefore kept the old failed gates and added a visible savings/F1-loss tradeoff instead of silently changing their outcomes. A selected separator format was frozen before 64 new MeeQA questions from 31 training-disjoint families. It saved 2.95% input and 1.97% total tokens; output growth raised dollar cost 0.59%. Overall F1 improved in the observed sample, while answerable-only F1 and recall worsened. Report every dimension: an aggregate gain can conceal a subgroup loss.

A reviewer found a quotation-restoration mismatch during the raw test arm. Registering a correction before encoded answers, retaining original frozen results, and scoring a separate symmetric workspace-policy audit preserved the comparison without pretending the original grader never had a defect. Correct source quotations should not have labels expanded twice. Grounding failures are rejected outputs, not successful abstentions and not evidence that the model never produced an ungrounded answer.

The plan's discovery call limit was a configurable default; user-directed continuation used an explicit immutable successor allocation within the unchanged global call/dollar limits. Preserve final-evaluation capacity transactionally and show the allocation in accounting rather than relabeling development calls as baselines.
